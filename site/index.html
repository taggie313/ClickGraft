ClickGraft

HP Click for Mac is built for Intel processors, so on a modern Mac it runs through a translator and crawls. ClickGraft makes a copy of your HP Click and puts the Apple Silicon engine into the copy.

Why HP Click is slow

In 2020 Apple started replacing the Intel processors in Macs with its own, called Apple Silicon. Your Mac still runs apps built for the older Intel chips by translating them as they go — that's Rosetta. HP Click is one of those apps, and that translation is why it takes a minute to start and why clicks take a moment to register.

Here is the part that makes this fixable: HP already builds the important parts of HP Click for Apple Silicon. Page layout, colour handling, the print engine — they are compiled for your Mac's processor and are sitting inside the app you have installed right now. What HP packages them with is the wrong engine: an Intel one.

ClickGraft swaps that one component for the Apple Silicon version, which HP downloads rather than writes. Everything else is left exactly as HP shipped it.

What it does to your Mac

  • Your HP Click is not modified. It is opened for reading only, and left exactly as it is.
  • You end up with two apps. Your original, and HP Click (Apple Silicon) beside it. Your printers and settings carry over.
  • To undo everything, drag the new app to the Trash. There is no uninstaller because there is nothing else to remove.
  • No HP software is distributed. ClickGraft contains none of HP's code. It works on the copy of HP Click you already installed yourself.

Before anything is written, the app shows you the whole plan: what it reads, what it creates, every change it makes and why, and every file it downloads with the fingerprint it checks each one against. Nothing happens until you press the button.

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon. Any M-series processor. On an Intel Mac there is nothing to fix.
  • Your own installed HP Click 4.8.117. ClickGraft ships no HP software and cannot get it for you.
  • Apple's Command Line Tools. A free download from Apple. Most Macs used for design or print work already have them, and if yours doesn't, macOS offers to install them the first time ClickGraft opens — accept, wait for it to finish, and open ClickGraft again.

Installing it

  1. Download ClickGraft.zip and unzip it.
  2. Drag ClickGraft.app wherever you like — your Applications folder is fine.
  3. Double-click it and follow the six screens.

Because it is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, there is no right-clicking to open it and no "unidentified developer" warning. macOS asks once whether you're sure you want to open something downloaded from the internet, and the very first launch takes a few extra seconds while macOS checks it with Apple. Both are normal and happen once.

Don't run both apps at once

The two apps share your printers and settings, and that sharing means opening one while the other is running makes the second one quit without saying anything — no window, no error. It looks like the app failed to start. Quit one before opening the other.

Which versions work

HP Click 4.8.117 today. ClickGraft refuses any version it has not been tested against rather than guessing, because it has to know exactly where its changes go and guessing would risk your app.

If you have a version it doesn't recognise, the app offers a button that writes a description of it. Open an issue with that report attached and support can be added.

Is this allowed?

ClickGraft distributes no HP code, and it makes no change to what HP Click is permitted to do. It doesn't touch licensing, supplies, features, or any restriction HP put in the software. It swaps one downloaded runtime component so the Apple Silicon code HP already wrote actually runs natively, and repairs a bug in HP's own files that throws an error on every launch — on Intel Macs too.

That narrow scope is deliberate and it is enforced on contributions. It's what keeps the project defensible and useful.
